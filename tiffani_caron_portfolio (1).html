Approach

Design starts where the source material runs out.

Most of my strongest work has started with a gap: graduate-level content built for the wrong audience, a rubric that faculty didn't trust, a lesson that only worked live and needed to survive on its own. The job is rarely "make it prettier." It's figuring out what the learner actually needs to walk away with, then building the scaffolding, sequencing, and feedback loops to get them there.

"I don't just translate content down a reading level. I restructure it around what the audience needs to do with it."

That's meant curating open-source material, career-exploration tools, and AI-assisted drafting into a single coherent course. It's also meant reframing the same instructional change three different ways for a director, a faculty member, and a room of skeptical peers. Below is a sample of that work.

Pre-College Program · Northeastern University Curriculum Redesign

Healthcare Professions Course

The Gap

The available graduate-level source material focused almost entirely on healthcare business operations — supply chain, logistics — which would have given high school learners a narrow, skewed view of the field instead of real exposure to the range of healthcare careers.

The Approach

Restructured the course around "healthcare as a system": facility types, insurance, how healthcare organizations function — then connected each piece to the professions working within it. Curated open-source textbook material and a career-exploration platform, and used AI-assisted drafting (reviewed and adapted for accuracy) to fill remaining content gaps. Because the course ran fully asynchronous, I built a self-directed career tracker so learners could log and reflect on their interests without live facilitation.

93% completion rate (181 of 194 enrolled)
36 Net Promoter Score — vs. 32 higher-ed avg.
4.7/5 rated understanding of material
76% wanted another course like it
